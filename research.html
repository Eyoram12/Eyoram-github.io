Research Profile

Research Overview

Applied epidemiological research focused on public health problems and the use of health data for decision-making.

Research Focus

My research interests are centered on field epidemiology, malaria epidemiology, disease surveillance, climate and health, temporal and seasonal disease patterns, and health data analysis.

I am particularly interested in applying quantitative epidemiological methods to understand disease patterns, identify important determinants, assess surveillance performance, and develop evidence that can support public health planning.

My current work includes retrospective studies, time-series analysis, climate-disease association analysis, lag-effect analysis, forecasting, and spatial analysis.

Research Approach

Evidence-Based Public Health Research

From Health Data to Public Health Evidence

My research approach emphasizes the systematic use of epidemiological and statistical methods to transform routine health and environmental data into useful evidence for public health decision-making.

This includes examining disease trends over time, identifying seasonal patterns, assessing relationships between climate variables and disease incidence, evaluating surveillance systems, and developing forecasting models.

The overall goal is to generate practical evidence that can contribute to disease prevention, surveillance, preparedness, and improved public health planning.
